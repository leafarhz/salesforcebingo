## Longer version, with the observation worth sharing

Klaus Ott Morrone gave his first-ever Dreamforce presentation this week — at his
first-ever Dreamforce.

Watching him up there took me straight back to myself, 13 years ago. Same nerves,
same enormous room, same quiet certainty that everyone else already knows each other
and you're the only one who doesn't.

That feeling is exactly what we built this for.

At the LATAM Community Meetup we ran Networking Bingo — a 5×5 card, one per person,
and you fill a square by finding someone it describes. No icebreaker circle, no
"turn to the person next to you". Just a reason to walk up to a stranger.

After 35 minutes:

→ 31 people played
→ 226 distinct introductions
→ 48% of every possible pairing in the room
→ 117 pairs confirmed from both sides
→ 3 blackouts — all 24 squares, meaning they met nearly everyone present
→ all 55 prompts claimed at least once, so no square was dead weight

But here's the thing I'll carry into the next one.

Every prompt you could settle by *glancing* at someone got hunted down:
laptop stickers (12), Trailhead superbadges (11), first-time attendees (10).

Every prompt that needed an actual conversation lagged behind:
*"has made a real friend through the Salesforce community"* — 3.
*"can explain the Enterprise AI Harness"* — 2.

Same room, same people, same 35 minutes. The format quietly rewarded speed over
depth. Next time I'd weight the harder prompts higher and let the room slow down.

(And yes — *"is wearing Salesforce or Trailblazer swag"* was claimed once. At
Dreamforce. Make of that what you will.)

If you want to run this at your own community event, it's open source. Built and run
entirely on Salesforce: a guest-access Experience Cloud site, scan a QR, enter your
email, play. No login, no app install, no accounts to manage.

https://github.com/leafarhz/salesforcebingo
